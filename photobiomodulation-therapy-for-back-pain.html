When Back Pain Slowly Becomes Part of Daily Life:

Back pain often begins unnoticed—a mild ache after a long day, stiffness on waking up, numbness, weakness or discomfort after sitting for hours. Over time, this occasional discomfort may become persistent, affecting posture, sleep, work, and movement. Back pain is one of the most common reasons people experience reduced quality of life.

The back supports the body, protects the spinal cord, and allows bending, lifting, and twisting. It is made up of bones, discs, muscles, ligaments, and nerves. When any of these structures are strained or irritated, back pain develops.

Conditions Commonly Associated With Back Pain:

Back pain may be linked to one or more of the following conditions:

  • Lumbar Spondylosis – Age-related wear and tear of the spine
  • Muscle Strain or Spasm – Due to overuse, poor posture, or sudden movement
  • Disc Bulge or Herniation or prolapse (L4 - L5) Pressure on spinal nerves
  • Sciatica – Pain radiating from the lower back to the leg due to nerve compression
  • Postural Back Pain – Prolonged sitting or improper body mechanics
  • Degenerative Disc Disease – Gradual breakdown of spinal discs
  • Facet Joint Syndrome – Pain arising from spinal joints
  • Stress-Related Back Pain – Muscle tightness linked to emotional stress

These conditions often develop gradually and may worsen without timely care.
